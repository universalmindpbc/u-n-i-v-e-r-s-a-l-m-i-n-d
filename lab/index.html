Electronic music and fashion as mindfulness

Making a beat. Designing a garment. Listening closely. Creating together. Universal Mind brings electronic music and fashion together as a mindfulness experience, using creativity to practise presence, self-awareness and connection.

Free access. Dissolving stigma. Clear pathways to support.

Dissolving Stigma
Music, fashion and shared activities give young people a natural way to start a conversation about mental health.
Transforming Access
Young people can take part online through the Universal Mind digital platform and watch live TV. The Linfox road train brings the festival, workshops and in-person support to regional communities.
Free to Participate
Young people can attend the festival and take part in its workshops for free.
Transforming Referral Pathways
Young people can join through a headspace referral, a school nomination or their own expression of interest. A welcome conversation connects them with optional on-site support and local services after the festival.

The shared support space brings together headspace, Médecins Sans Frontières (MSF) and the Australian Association of Buddhist Counsellors and Psychotherapists (AABCAP).

First Nations-led fashion runway

We propose two shows in partnership with the Australian Indigenous Mentoring Experience (AIME). Iris van Herpen opens with sculptural dresses, followed by Nagnata movement wear. Billie-Jean Hamlet and Cindy Rostron inform the casting direction.

I Am Portal

Seven geometric workshop spaces for education, intervention and interactivity, with ten people in each space.

Inspired by Life Be In It and the idea of taking health promotion on the road, Universal Mind brings a new focus: mental health. First and foremost for youth aged 16–24, then for all Australians, the Lab brings creativity, learning and support into the heart of local communities.

The vision brings CSIRO technology, Universal Mind, headspace and New South Wales Education together as one, shaped in collaboration with the communities we visit.

This is creativity as mindfulness.

Learn STEAM — science, technology, engineering, arts and mathematics — while experiencing world-class DJs, artists and bands, together as one. Make music on smart devices, explore responsive technology and learn by creating.

Learn fashion skills, design a runway show and take the first steps towards your own fashion label, on the day. Indigenous fashion, local creativity and collaboration run through the experience.

Change your emotions. Change your world.

Through Apple Watch biofeedback, participants explore emotional regulation as the light and sound around them respond.

Apple Watch heart-rhythm data flows through Immersion Neuroscience’s SIX platform. Inferences related to oxytocin, dopamine and cortisol guide changes in light, colour, motion and sound. Grounding, breath and connection become part of a responsive environment for practising mindfulness and emotional regulation.

The watch records cardiac signals; the neurochemical interpretation is inferred. Apple Watch, teamLab, TDC and Arup form the partnership direction for the experience.

  1. Connection to our earth. Feel your feet on the ground, or the support beneath you as you sit.
  2. Connection to self. Feel your breath. Feel your body.
  3. Connection to each other. Feel the energy of the people around you.

From anxiety to peace of mind: a Universal Mind journey through connection to our earth, ourselves and each other.

A travelling lab. A lasting local connection.

A Linfox road train carries the Lab into a green space beside the water. A vaulted main pavilion, sculptural food and play areas, a fashion runway and individual listening spaces bring the programme together. Moroso Tropicália furniture by Patricia Urquiola creates places to gather and pause.

Local collaboration shapes the architecture, materials, activities and ongoing use. Where a community chooses to retain facilities, the ambition is a continuing resource for learning, mental health and connection.

The interactive biofeedback demonstration collects no health data. Applications are not collected on this page.

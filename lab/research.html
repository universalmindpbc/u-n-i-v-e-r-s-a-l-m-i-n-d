Mindfulness Biofeedback / AR / VR / Mixed Reality

The evidence supports further research. Heart-rate variability feedback has reduced stress or anxiety in controlled studies. The added value of live heart-rate or EEG feedback depends on the practice and what researchers compare it with. The proposed Lab experience still needs its own youth trial.

Three findings

  1. Live feedback can change how present people feel. In a 35-person VR study, participants who saw and heard their own physiological signals reported greater presence and relaxation than those receiving a replay. A 60-person randomised trial found comparable results with and without a live pulse display during the same self-compassion practice. Live versus replayed feedback · Self-compassion trial
  2. People can feel more engaged without a greater improvement in mood. In the Healium AR study, mood improved in both groups. Participants using live EEG reported greater engagement. Across 16 randomised training studies, a separate meta-analysis found a small benefit for psychological distress and no reliable advantage for mindfulness itself. Healium AR trial · EEG meta-analysis
  3. Shared feedback can support a sense of connection. In a study of pairs, participants sharing breathing and EEG feedback reported greater empathy and social presence. A separate group installation used heart rhythms to change sound, light and vibration. The first measured a shared experience. The second demonstrates a design. Whether a young festival audience feels a lasting sense of belonging remains an open question. Paired meditation study · Resident Thrum installation
How strong is the evidence?

The 63 publications include at least 15 primary reports with random allocation, alongside feasibility studies, reviews and design papers. Some randomised trials tested digital or community support without a live feedback signal. In the 188-person inpatient trial, an independent psychiatrist assessed anxiety while masked to allocation. Participants and therapists knew the intervention. Masking is unverified in several other reports. A corpus-wide double-blind count remains undetermined. Inpatient trial methods

A meta-analysis of 24 HRV studies found lower stress and anxiety versus controls. A review of 18 remote HRV studies found benefits for depression and HRV, with an inconclusive pooled result for stress. The consumer EEG review found no sham-feedback control in its randomised training trials. Expectation and attention may therefore explain part of the observed benefit. These reviews supplement the 63-publication register. HRV meta-analysis · Remote HRV meta-analysis · EEG meta-analysis

What has Healium tested?

Five distinct journal reports were identified. Three documented live EEG feedback: the AR mindfulness trial, a four-firefighter pilot and a 100-person study of hospital staff. The staff study allocated participants alternately to VR with EEG feedback or audio meditation, so immersion and feedback varied together. The nature-VR study recorded EEG before and after meditation. The 33-person blood-donor pilot assessed mood before and after VR. An Apple Watch heart-rate intervention remains unverified in these five reports.

Youth, Defence, Indigenous and refugee evidence

One game study recruited 47 participants aged 18–24. Student trials include young adults with mixed or incompletely reported ages. An Australian Defence Force study tested delivery with 189 trainees. A 342-soldier randomised trial found no overall preventive effect on post-deployment PTSD. An Australian pilot with 20 Aboriginal young people aged 7–16 measured HRV during a culturally informed VR practice. In Kampala, 335 refugee youth aged 16–25 participated in a VR trial. Self-compassion improved, while depression outcomes were comparable to control. Neither youth study established an effect of live wearable feedback.

Ages 18–24 · Australian Defence Force · National Guard · Aboriginal youth in NSW · Refugee youth in Kampala

Studies in Tanzania, Lebanon and Türkiye inform delivery through local facilitators or accessible digital formats. These settings still need direct evaluation of the proposed road-train experience, including access in communities facing extreme poverty. Tanzania · Lebanon · Türkiye

Which signals drove live feedback?

A signal counts when it changed something the participant saw, heard or felt during the activity. A recording taken only for analysis stays outside that count. One publication can appear in several categories.

A derived indicator combines or interprets signals, for example a stress estimate or a synchrony score. The proprietary scores form a subset of these indicators. Two reports explicitly document HeartMath coherence. A further veteran trial used an Inner Balance sensor, but the precise score returned to its participants remains unconfirmed. The other documented inputs included breathing, skin conductance and movement.

Participant studies with identified live feedback (28)
  1. Brief Interactive Virtual Reality Mindfulness Training with Real-Time Biofeedback for Anxiety Reduction: A Pilot Study

    Live HRV drove changes in fog, sound and visuals.

  2. Enhancing Mindfulness Training in Virtual Reality: A Randomized Trial of Biofeedback Integration

  3. Using a Biofeedback-Based Mindfulness Practice System to Enhance Mindfulness and Alleviate Anxiety in College Students: A Randomized Controlled Trial

    The MindJourney system returned respiratory feedback. Skin conductance was a measurement outcome.

  4. The efficacy of virtual reality-enhanced EEG biofeedback in anxiety treatment: analysis of a digital health intervention

    EEG training supplied neurofeedback in the intervention. Clinical anxiety ratings were outcomes.

  5. ZenG

    Relative alpha-band EEG changed the tree colour. Twelve adults took part in informal prototype testing.

  6. Neurological Evidence of Diverse Self-Help Breathing Training With Virtual Reality and Biofeedback Assistance: Extensive Exploration Study of Electroencephalography Markers

    A respiration belt controlled the meadow. The investigators recorded EEG to compare the practice conditions.

  7. Effects on Mood and EEG States After Meditation in Augmented Reality With and Without Adjunctive Neurofeedback

    Frontal gamma asymmetry moved a glowing ball and determined when the AR story progressed.

  8. Mindfulness Development via Biofeedback for Anxiety in Hospitalized Patients: A Randomized Controlled Trial

    Auditory feedback followed EEG alpha power. A separate training stage varied audiovisual feedback with current heart rate. A psychiatrist masked to allocation rated anxiety.

  9. The Applications of the Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy and HRV Biofeedback in Modern Psychotherapy

    The case describes a participant viewing heart-rhythm changes and HRV during sessions.

  10. Biofeedback-Enhanced Virtual Reality Training for Stress Regulation: Concept and Pilot Study Results

    The field of view changed with a stress level derived from HRV in a seven-person pilot.

  11. A mobile VR-based respiratory biofeedback game to foster diaphragmatic breathing

    A controller on the abdomen sensed movement caused by breathing and fed it into the game.

  12. Use of Biofeedback-Based Virtual Reality in Pediatric Perioperative and Postoperative Settings: Observational Study

    Inner Balance and emWave presented a coherence score calculated from beat-to-beat intervals.

  13. Mindfulness-based Stress Reduction in VR: Visualising Meditation Performance using Real-time EEG Neurofeedback

    The authors describe a preliminary evaluation in which an EEG-derived relaxation estimate drove visual feedback. The accessible abstract gives limited detail about the calculation.

  14. Ego-depletion and motor skill performance under pressure—experimental effects of a short term virtual-reality based mindfulness breathing meditation with integrated biofeedback

    Abdominal movement during exhalation changed the virtual environment. The testing staff were masked to condition.

  15. HeartBit: mindful control of heart rate using haptic biofeedback

    The handheld device reproduced a measured heartbeat through touch.

  16. Attention Regulation Framework

    The evaluated interfaces tracked deliberate physical movements to support attention.

  17. Using a virtual reality game to train biofeedback-based regulation under stress conditions

  18. Development of a modular stress management platform (Performance Edge VR) and a pilot efficacy trial of a bio-feedback enhanced training module for controlled breathing

    A respiratory belt provided controlled-breathing feedback.

  19. Virtual reality versus 2D biofeedback for children and adolescents with stress related disorders: a randomized controlled trial

    A baseline heart-rate threshold guided feedback in both the 2D and VR training.

  20. Designing and Utilizing Biofeedback Games for Emotion Regulation

    In Nevermind, the game estimated arousal from beat-to-beat variability. The report also illustrates heart-rate recordings separately.

  21. Evaluation of a Virtual Reality Platform to Train Stress Management Skills for a Defense Workforce: Multisite, Mixed Methods Feasibility Study

    A live respiratory trace provided feedback on controlled breathing in ADF training.

  22. Heart Rate Variability and Cognitive Bias Feedback Interventions to Prevent Post-deployment PTSD: Results from a Randomized Controlled Trial

    The on-site emWave games responded to HRV. The home prompt provided separate paced-breathing practice.

  23. A Pilot Study of a Three-Session Heart Rate Variability Biofeedback Intervention for Veterans with Posttraumatic Stress Disorder

    The veteran waitlist pilot used clinical HRV biofeedback and a smartphone with a sensor for home practice.

  24. Using biofeedback while immersed in a stressful videogame increases the effectiveness of stress management skills in soldiers

    The soldiers’ game combined heart rate and skin conductance. The coach adjusted each signal’s weight in the stress feedback.

  25. Virtual reality experiences for breathing and relaxation training: The effects of real vs. placebo biofeedback

  26. Receiving self-compassion in the virtual world: Testing the potential value of biofeedback

  27. Augmented Virtual Reality Meditation

    Pairs saw their respiratory synchrony and a comparison of EEG frontal asymmetry. HRV and skin conductance were also recorded for analysis.

  28. Feeling-the-Beat: Enhancing Empathy and Engagement During Public Speaking Through Heart Rate Sharing

    The system shared heart rate between speakers and audience members. The comparison used fabricated or absent signals.

“Direct response” connects the programme to documented needs and access barriers. “Inspired by” connects its design to relevant research. The collection includes supportive, mixed and null findings; it does not establish the effects of the combined Lab experience.

Age ranges and study methods vary. Each paper retains its findings and limitations in Informatics. The mantra section records research context; no new mantra paper met this collection’s selected-methods inclusion threshold.

Sound, music and brain entrainment

This scorecard presents Universal Mind’s assessment of research informing the wider Universal Mind project. Our initial clinical focus is anxiety and PTSD, alongside research into music, auditory beats and physiological responses.

The B grade reflects this wider evidence. Research into gene expression is discussed separately below.

Why B?

Systematic reviews report benefits for anxiety and promising findings for PTSD symptoms. Results vary across interventions and study designs. Brain-entrainment and frequency-specific studies add physiological findings, while their clinical applications still need stronger evidence.

The grade applies to the research described here. It does not establish the effectiveness of every sound intervention or a particular Universal Mind experience.

Key studies

  1. 2025 · eClinicalMedicine

    Music therapy for the treatment of anxiety: a systematic review with multilevel meta-analyses

    Systematic review and meta-analysis · 51 studies

    Music therapy was associated with reduced self-reported anxiety. Physiological effects were small and not statistically significant.

    Why it matters: A substantial synthesis supporting music therapy for anxiety.

    Limit: The review concerns music therapy, with varied interventions and study quality. Its findings do not establish equivalent effects from every form of music listening.

  2. 2024 · Journal of Mood & Anxiety Disorders

    Music interventions for posttraumatic stress disorder: A systematic review

    Systematic review · 14 studies

    Thirteen of the fourteen studies reported reduced PTSD symptoms following music interventions.

    Why it matters: Directly relevant to Universal Mind’s initial focus on PTSD.

    Limit: Nine studies were feasibility studies. Small samples, weak controls and varied delivery limit confidence in the treatment effect.

  3. 2024 · Neuroscience

    Brain wave modulation and EEG power changes during auditory beats stimulation

    Comparative EEG study · 28 participants

    Four minutes of isochronic-derived auditory stimulation produced greater changes in gamma-band EEG power than gamma binaural beats and white noise, alongside a distinct alpha response.

    Why it matters: Measures how different auditory stimuli affect brain activity.

    Limit: Short-term EEG changes do not establish clinical benefit. The stimulation differed from conventional fixed-carrier isochronic tones, and gene expression was not measured.

  4. 2018 · Health

    Effect of 528 Hz Music on the Endocrine System and Autonomic Nervous System

    Small crossover study · 9 participants

    The study reported short-term changes in salivary oxytocin, cortisol and mood after listening to music labelled 528 Hz.

    Why it matters: An exploratory study connecting music exposure with hormonal measurements.

    Limit: Nine participants and brief exposure. Changes within one listening condition do not establish an advantage over the comparison music. OXTR transcription was not measured.

  5. 2026 · EXPLORE

    Efficacy of theta binaural beat therapy on pain, cognition and anxiety in adults: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

    Systematic review and meta-analysis · 13 randomized trials

    The review found reduced pain in a pooled analysis of four trials. Anxiety benefits appeared mainly in procedural and chronic-pain settings; some studies also reported episodic-memory benefits.

    Why it matters: Examines theta binaural beats across clinical and cognitive outcomes.

    Limit: Pooled pain evidence was low certainty. Anxiety and cognition findings were too varied to pool, and the review does not establish PTSD efficacy.

Sound and gene expression

Research already connects music with measured gene-expression changes. The studies below illustrate this work; they do not yet establish a consistent molecular mechanism or a gene-expression basis for the B grade.

2005 · Medical Science Monitor

Recreational music-making modulates the human stress response: a preliminary individualized gene expression strategy

In 32 participants, researchers measured 45 immune-response genes in blood during a stress-and-recovery experiment. More markers reversed the stress-associated pattern after recreational music-making than after rest or continued stress.

Limit: A small, preliminary study of a specific music-making intervention.

2025 · Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience

Music elicits different gene expression responses in the buccal cavity of age-related cognitive disorders patients and healthy controls

Brief music exposure was associated with different gene-expression responses in oral tissue from people with cognitive disorders and healthy controls.

Limit: Exploratory findings in peripheral tissue do not establish changes in brain tissue or therapeutic efficacy.

Research opportunities

Larger controlled trials can test whether these molecular findings replicate, how long they last, and how they relate to clinical outcomes. Measuring gene expression alongside symptoms, hormones and physiology can help distinguish the effects of music-making, listening and auditory-beat stimulation.

OXTR and the wider candidate network provide targets for future investigation. Their involvement remains a research question.

Anxiety and PTSD are our starting points. We welcome researchers investigating other conditions, populations and applications.

How we assess the evidence

Universal Mind’s A–C grades summarise the evidence for a defined intervention and clinical focus. They consider systematic reviews, controlled trials, clinical guidance, consistency, study limitations and relevance to the intended use.

A
Supported by current clinical guidance and multiple controlled evidence syntheses.
B
Positive systematic evidence, with meaningful heterogeneity or a product translation gap.
C
Promising or indirect evidence. The proposed delivery itself is not yet established.

Grades describe the research basis, not a medical recommendation.

The medical-cross trust scores in the chapters identify study designs: three for double-blind randomized trials, two for single-blind randomized trials, and one for general research. The letter grade summarises the wider evidence and is not an average of those scores.

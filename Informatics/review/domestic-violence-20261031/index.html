Domestic & family violence research

This collection brings together 117 publications on domestic and family violence and related trauma: 51 reports from randomised controlled trials, 25 reviews and meta-analyses, and 41 other research reports. It includes 10 involving EMDR, nine on mindfulness or meditation, and eight on music-based approaches, alongside talking therapies, parent–child programmes and violence prevention. Each summary explains what was found, who was studied and the limits of the evidence.

These are publication counts; several reports can come from the same study. The approach counts highlight selected topics rather than dividing the whole collection into separate categories.

AI publication recommendation: 117 qualified summaries. The audit covers all 130 assessed records. Thirteen are withheld from this release.

The recommendation concerns accurate research summaries, not 117 proven treatments. It includes negative results, reviews and accounts of people’s experiences. This is AI editorial work; no clinical peer review or human paper-by-paper approval is claimed.

Dot’s original 116 meant “ready for review.” After applying later evidence, this audit recommends 109 of those original records plus eight later additions. Of the resulting 117, 115 are new publications and two update existing library entries.

  • What was sound: most summaries distinguish symptom changes from reduced violence, preserve null results, and label population and source-access limits.
  • What needed changing: five benefit or safety summaries required substantive qualification. Further methods updates and a distinction between two distress measures are now incorporated.
  • What is withheld: ten records with unresolved methods, numerical, sample or correction questions; one whose substantive claims could not be independently checked; and two master’s theses outside this journal-publication release.

Assessment method, limits and preserved history

One cross identifies a randomized trial design supported by the accessed source. An abstract can establish the reported design without verifying allocation concealment or its execution. No two-cross, double-blind trial is verified in this release. Reviews receive their own label and no inherited trial crosses. Crosses do not mean benefit, low risk of bias or human approval.

Direct evidence studies domestic, family or intimate-partner violence. Context and related evidence have stated transfer limits. Neither publication counts nor pooled reviews count as independent replications; the exact independent-study total remains unresolved.

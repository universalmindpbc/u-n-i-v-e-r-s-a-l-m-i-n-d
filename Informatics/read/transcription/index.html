Transcription Atlas

Our priority is transcription factor binding sites in the DNA that regulates the oxytocin receptor gene, OXTR.

Understanding Binding Sites and our approach

A transcription factor is a protein that recognises particular DNA sequences and helps regulate gene activity. A binding site is a short stretch of DNA where that recognition takes place.

The DNA sites are illustrative. The arrows on them indicate motif orientation along DNA. The Beta diagram presents the Atlas pattern beside the biological idea; the mapping is the research step ahead.

The 15 sites, ENCODE clusters and predicted matches

The 15 sites

One OXTR promoter study reported 15 candidate binding locations using sequence analysis and laboratory binding tests. This is one study’s set, not a total for every region and cell type.

An ENCODE cluster

An experimental neighbourhood where a protein was detected associating with DNA. It usually covers a wider region than the exact binding sequence. The underlying experiments and cell types are linked in the catalogue.

A predicted match

A computer finds a DNA sequence resembling a protein’s preferred binding pattern. It is a candidate location to investigate; matching the pattern alone does not demonstrate binding in a cell.

Several predictions and experimental regions can overlap the same piece of DNA. These are complementary types of evidence, so their counts remain separate.

Mapping & verification

Fixed human OXTR window: GRCh38 chr3:8,740,380–8,779,628 (0-based, end-exclusive). The direction follows OXTR’s minus strand, from high to low genomic coordinates. The origin is the window edge, not the transcription start site.

The line and full circle retain interval widths. For M9, one central base represents each interval within this window. An even-width interval uses the first central base in the OXTR direction. Regions extending beyond the window are clipped for display; their original coordinates remain in every export.

d = floor((offset_start + offset_end − 1) / 2) Full circle = d / 39,248 turns · M9 = d mod 9 Zero is displayed as 9. All circles start at north and run clockwise. Compression merges positions; node counts expose those overlaps.

The Atlas reference keeps its original grid and phase: Alpha, Gamma and Epsilon use 24 period positions; Beta uses nine residue positions. The shared circle aligns both layers at north, clockwise, with no fitted rotation. On the full-position circle, one DNA window fills one turn. On the line, Atlas turn × window length gives the guide position; only node positions are shown, without connecting curves. In M9, the DNA has nine residue positions while each Atlas guide retains its native grid. A shared angle does not equate a period index with a residue. The Atlas paths show geometry, not measured movement or binding order.

The browser filters and draws precomputed results. Python/Biopython and Wolfram are used in the reproducible preparation and verification, rather than called as live services for each selection.

Which evidence matters most?

Start with a positive binding result at a defined DNA sequence. “Tested” alone does not tell us whether binding happened.

Binding detected at a defined sequenceA positive binding result at a specified DNA sequence is the most direct anchor for a location comparison. Check the protein, assay and biological context.

Protein association detected across a regionENCODE and BrainTF supply experimental support over a stretch of DNA. Resolve the site within it and inspect the contributing cell types and experiments.

Sequence match predictedJASPAR matches are candidates for exploration and experimental follow-up. A model match alone does not establish binding at that location.

Did it change gene activity? That is a separate result, recorded alongside binding. A relevant cell type, good experimental controls and replication strengthen the evidence.

A paper can report any of these evidence types. These priorities describe biological support; the significance of a geometric match needs its own statistical test.

What the evidence filters mean

Laboratory tested includes positive, negative and mixed results. Binding evidence includes both defined sequence elements and regional occupancy. Functional evidence describes a measured regulatory response.

A record can appear in several evidence filters. Original catalogue labels remain in Methods & interpretation and the exports.

Laboratory tested

The record reports a laboratory assay. Includes positive, mixed and negative results; a group-level test does not resolve every member.

Binding evidence

Reported DNA binding or regional occupancy, at the resolution and in the system stated by the source.

Functional evidence

An element, region or element-based reporter has an experimental regulatory response. This does not imply every nucleotide or predicted motif was independently validated.

Annotation or prediction

Sequence or database annotation; direct binding is not established by the annotation alone.

Regulatory context

Evidence concerning gene regulation without an independently resolved binding site in this record.

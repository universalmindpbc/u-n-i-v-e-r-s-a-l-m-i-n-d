Two-year follow up of a cluster randomised controlled trial for women experiencing intimate partner violence: effect of screening and family doctor-delivered counselling on quality of life, mental and physical health and abuse exposure.

Hegarty K; Valpied J; Taft A; Brown SJ; Gold L; Gunn J; O'Doherty L · BMJ open · 2020 · Cluster randomized trial follow-up

Original publication · Original publication · Original publication · Original publication · Original publication · Original publication

Recorded summary

At 24 months the WEAVE counselling offer did not improve primary or secondary outcomes versus usual care. The 12-month secondary depression finding did not persist; these reports are one cohort.

Recorded limitations

Only 166/272 responded at 24 months and 49% of women offered counselling attended any (mostlyone session). Self-reported outcomes were unmasked. The absence of statistical benefit is not equivalence; within-group improvement does not establish counselling efficacy.

Evidence and provenance

Chapter Trust Factor uses a minimum of three distinct qualifying studies. Publication records retain their own evidence and assessment.

quality
risk Of Bias
Unmasked self-report, low counselling uptake, substantial long-term attrition and selected English-speaking screening responders limit inference. Masked researchers/analysts and cluster-adjusted analyses support methods; no formal numerical risk rating.
attrition
24-monthresponse 81/137 (59%)intervention and 85/135 (63%)control, 166/272 overall. Available-data mixed models do not remove potential nonresponse bias.
confounding
Mixed-effects models use available repeated observations, adjusted for practice clustering and urban/rural stratification. Nonattender exclusions are selected analyses. Both arms received information/resources and repeated IPV questionnaires.
adverse Findings
Report says no significant harms detected and refers to the dedicated harms analysis. A same-family primary abstract nevertheless describes short-/long-term negative emotions, recall, retraumatization and minimization. These are participation experiences, not demonstrated treatment-caused excess harm; counts and causal attribution unavailable. 24-month VAS difference nonsignificant.
transfer Limits
English-speaking women who returned a mailed screen and reported partner fear; young women and those with lower education/employment were under-represented. No basis for a child/adolescent-specific conclusion.

quality
risk Of Bias
Unmasked self-report, low counselling uptake, substantial long-term attrition and selected English-speaking screening responders limit inference. Masked researchers/analysts and cluster-adjusted analyses support methods; no formal numerical risk rating.
attrition
24-monthresponse 81/137 (59%)intervention and 85/135 (63%)control, 166/272 overall. Available-data mixed models do not remove potential nonresponse bias.
confounding
Mixed-effects models use available repeated observations, adjusted for practice clustering and urban/rural stratification. Nonattender exclusions are selected analyses. Both arms received information/resources and repeated IPV questionnaires.
adverse Findings
Report says no significant harms detected and refers to the dedicated harms analysis. A same-family primary abstract nevertheless describes short-/long-term negative emotions, recall, retraumatization and minimization. These are participation experiences, not demonstrated treatment-caused excess harm; counts and causal attribution unavailable. 24-month VAS difference nonsignificant.
transfer Limits
English-speaking women who returned a mailed screen and reported partner fear; young women and those with lower education/employment were under-represented. No basis for a child/adolescent-specific conclusion.

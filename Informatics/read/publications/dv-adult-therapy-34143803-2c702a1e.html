Evaluation of an integrated intervention to reduce psychological distress and intimate partner violence in refugees: Results from the Nguvu cluster randomized feasibility trial.

Greene MC; Likindikoki S; Rees S; Bonz A; Kaysen D; Misinzo L; Njau T; Kiluwa S; Turner R; Ventevogel P; Mbwambo JKK; Tol WA · PloS one · 2021 · Cluster randomized feasibility trial with recruitment after cluster allocation

Original publication · Original publication · Original publication · Original publication · Original publication

Recorded summary

Nguvu was feasible for some refugee women, but the adjusted randomized comparison does not clearly establish psychological or IPV benefit. Severe IPV predicted lower completion; an exploratory subgroup showed higher subsequent IPV. No long-term durability was tested.

Recorded limitations

Under-recruitment, baseline imbalances, recruitment after cluster assignment, unmasked self-report, vulnerable assessor masking, complete-case analysis and severity-related intervention dropout limit causal inference. Table 1 reverses the narrative cluster-arm counts; all 43 clusters and individual 158/153 totals are retained.

Evidence and provenance

quality
risk Of Bias
Important concerns from baseline mental-health/IPV imbalance, post-allocation recruitment, unmasked self-report and possible assessor unmasking; no formal numeric rating.
attrition
36/311 (11.6%) lacked endline: 15/158 Nguvu, 21/153 usual care. Eighteen relocated/resettled. Later resettlement stages predicted loss. One withdrew fearing neighbors would learn of participation. Intervention completion 92/158 is distinct from study follow-up 143/158.
confounding
Models 1–3 labeled ITT by authors are complete-case randomized-group comparisons, not all 311 participants. Model 3 controls baseline imbalances; favorable per-protocol PTSD estimate is selected. Moderator tests are exploratory, multiple and underpowered.
adverse Findings
Three adverse events reviewed by DSMB/IRB were judged unlikely due to participation and consistent with ongoing IPV; participants were connected to services. Exploratory high-baseline-IPV group had higher subsequent IPV frequency with Nguvu; not causal proof. One privacy-related withdrawal.
transfer Limits
Adult Congolese women in established refugee groups with past-year physical/sexual IPV and elevated distress. Imminent suicidality/severe psychiatric impairment excluded; no durability beyond nine weeks.

    finding
    Nguvu was feasible for some refugee women, but the adjusted randomized comparison does not clearly establish psychological or IPV benefit. Severe IPV predicted lower completion; an exploratory subgroup showed higher subsequent IPV. No long-term durability was tested.
    limitation
    Under-recruitment, baseline imbalances, recruitment after cluster assignment, unmasked self-report, vulnerable assessor masking, complete-case analysis and severity-related intervention dropout limit causal inference. Table 1 reverses the narrative cluster-arm counts; all 43 clusters and individual 158/153 totals are retained.

    risk Of Bias
    Important concerns from baseline mental-health/IPV imbalance, post-allocation recruitment, unmasked self-report and possible assessor unmasking; no formal numeric rating.
    attrition
    36/311 (11.6%) lacked endline: 15/158 Nguvu, 21/153 usual care. Eighteen relocated/resettled. Later resettlement stages predicted loss. One withdrew fearing neighbors would learn of participation. Intervention completion 92/158 is distinct from study follow-up 143/158.
    confounding
    Models 1–3 labeled ITT by authors are complete-case randomized-group comparisons, not all 311 participants. Model 3 controls baseline imbalances; favorable per-protocol PTSD estimate is selected. Moderator tests are exploratory, multiple and underpowered.
    adverse Findings
    Three adverse events reviewed by DSMB/IRB were judged unlikely due to participation and consistent with ongoing IPV; participants were connected to services. Exploratory high-baseline-IPV group had higher subsequent IPV frequency with Nguvu; not causal proof. One privacy-related withdrawal.
    transfer Limits
    Adult Congolese women in established refugee groups with past-year physical/sexual IPV and elevated distress. Imminent suicidality/severe psychiatric impairment excluded; no durability beyond nine weeks.

quality
risk Of Bias
Important concerns from baseline mental-health/IPV imbalance, post-allocation recruitment, unmasked self-report and possible assessor unmasking; no formal numeric rating.
attrition
36/311 (11.6%) lacked endline: 15/158 Nguvu, 21/153 usual care. Eighteen relocated/resettled. Later resettlement stages predicted loss. One withdrew fearing neighbors would learn of participation. Intervention completion 92/158 is distinct from study follow-up 143/158.
confounding
Models 1–3 labeled ITT by authors are complete-case randomized-group comparisons, not all 311 participants. Model 3 controls baseline imbalances; favorable per-protocol PTSD estimate is selected. Moderator tests are exploratory, multiple and underpowered.
adverse Findings
Three adverse events reviewed by DSMB/IRB were judged unlikely due to participation and consistent with ongoing IPV; participants were connected to services. Exploratory high-baseline-IPV group had higher subsequent IPV frequency with Nguvu; not causal proof. One privacy-related withdrawal.
transfer Limits
Adult Congolese women in established refugee groups with past-year physical/sexual IPV and elevated distress. Imminent suicidality/severe psychiatric impairment excluded; no durability beyond nine weeks.

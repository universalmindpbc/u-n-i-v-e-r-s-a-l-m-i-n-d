Problem-solving therapy for pregnant women experiencing depressive symptoms and intimate partner violence: A randomised, controlled feasibility trial in rural Ethiopia.

Keynejad RC; Bitew T; Sorsdahl K; Myers B; Honikman S; Medhin G; Deyessa N; Mulushoa A; Fekadu E; Howard LM; Hanlon C · PLOS global public health · 2023 · Randomized controlled feasibility study with material allocation-protocol deviations

Original publication · Original publication · Original publication · Original publication · Original publication

Recorded summary

PST-IPV was implementable with adaptations, but serious allocation errors, 25% missing follow-up and short descriptive assessment preclude an efficacy conclusion. One serious assault event was reported without arm or causal attribution.

Recorded limitations

Seventeen women with psychological/emotional IPV had no chance of assignment toPST-IPV; baseline imbalance suggests possible nonrandom allocation. Twelve of 39 assessments occurred before 9 weeks. Underpowered feasibility, missing follow-up, provider fidelity problems and overlapping IPV recall periods limit interpretation.

quality
risk Of Bias
High concern for comparative causal inference because 17/52 entered the wrong allocation table and arm-closing rules generated uneven groups; masked assessors/analysts do not repair this. No formal RoB/Jadad score.
attrition
13/52 (25%)missing follow-up: 5 moved, 4 untraceable, 4 declined (often postnatal confinement). Arm follow-up 19/25, 8/12, 12/15. Nonresponders younger (median 20 vs 25.5) and apparently more trauma-symptomatic. 12/39 assessments occurred 37–61 days rather than target 63.
confounding
Three-arm intended sample 35 and full sample 52 are reported separately. Shared controls with linked depression trial must not be counted as independent evidence. No powered efficacy test; differences in clinical medians are not treatment effects.
adverse Findings
One serious adverse event was assault by husband; assignment and treatment relatedness unreported. Two obstructed/prolonged-labour complications among 29 delivered. Independent assessors found risk/confidentiality discussions needed improvement, and some providers did not respond to IPV disclosure.
transfer Limits
Rural Amharic-speaking pregnant women with depressive symptoms and IPV; imminent risk/psychosis/emergency care excluded. No long-term follow-up, no established adolescent subgroup effect.

    finding
    PST-IPV was implementable with adaptations, but serious allocation errors, 25% missing follow-up and short descriptive assessment preclude an efficacy conclusion. One serious assault event was reported without arm or causal attribution.
    limitation
    Seventeen women with psychological/emotional IPV had no chance of assignment toPST-IPV; baseline imbalance suggests possible nonrandom allocation. Twelve of 39 assessments occurred before 9 weeks. Underpowered feasibility, missing follow-up, provider fidelity problems and overlapping IPV recall periods limit interpretation.
    design
    type
    Randomized controlled feasibility study with material allocation-protocol deviations
    randomization Evidence
    Independent statistician generated 3-arm 2: 1: 1 table; coordinator/research assistant retained tables and received calls after baseline. Enrollment in an arm closed at 25. Seventeen of 52 women misclassified as no IPV were instead allocated by linked trial’s 2-arm table, excluding their opportunity forPST-IPV; authors note possible nonrandom allocation from baseline imbalance.
    masking
    participants
    No participant masking procedure established; active sessions versus leaflet care differ.
    providers
    Explicitly unmasked; separate provider groups trained for each active intervention.
    assessors
    Independent collectors exchanged recruitment sites for follow-up and were masked, with unmasking incidents to be documented; no incident count reported.
    analysts
    Explicitly masked to allocation.
    crosses

    cross Rationale
    Random tables and controlled allocation are verified, but one third were routed through the wrong allocation scheme. Retain one design cross with this prominent qualification; no double-blind claim and no reliable efficacy inference.
    quality
    risk Of Bias
    High concern for comparative causal inference because 17/52 entered the wrong allocation table and arm-closing rules generated uneven groups; masked assessors/analysts do not repair this. No formal RoB/Jadad score.
    attrition
    13/52 (25%)missing follow-up: 5 moved, 4 untraceable, 4 declined (often postnatal confinement). Arm follow-up 19/25, 8/12, 12/15. Nonresponders younger (median 20 vs 25.5) and apparently more trauma-symptomatic. 12/39 assessments occurred 37–61 days rather than target 63.
    confounding
    Three-arm intended sample 35 and full sample 52 are reported separately. Shared controls with linked depression trial must not be counted as independent evidence. No powered efficacy test; differences in clinical medians are not treatment effects.
    adverse Findings
    One serious adverse event was assault by husband; assignment and treatment relatedness unreported. Two obstructed/prolonged-labour complications among 29 delivered. Independent assessors found risk/confidentiality discussions needed improvement, and some providers did not respond to IPV disclosure.
    transfer Limits
    Rural Amharic-speaking pregnant women with depressive symptoms and IPV; imminent risk/psychosis/emergency care excluded. No long-term follow-up, no established adolescent subgroup effect.

13/52 (25%)missing follow-up: 5 moved, 4 untraceable, 4 declined (often postnatal confinement). Arm follow-up 19/25, 8/12, 12/15. Nonresponders younger (median 20 vs 25.5) and apparently more trauma-symptomatic. 12/39 assessments occurred 37–61 days rather than target 63.
confounding
Three-arm intended sample 35 and full sample 52 are reported separately. Shared controls with linked depression trial must not be counted as independent evidence. No powered efficacy test; differences in clinical medians are not treatment effects.
adverse Findings
One serious adverse event was assault by husband; assignment and treatment relatedness unreported. Two obstructed/prolonged-labour complications among 29 delivered. Independent assessors found risk/confidentiality discussions needed improvement, and some providers did not respond to IPV disclosure.
transfer Limits
Rural Amharic-speaking pregnant women with depressive symptoms and IPV; imminent risk/psychosis/emergency care excluded. No long-term follow-up, no established adolescent subgroup effect.

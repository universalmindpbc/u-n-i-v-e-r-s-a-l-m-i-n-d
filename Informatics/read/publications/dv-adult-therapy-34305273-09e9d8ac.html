A Randomized Controlled Trial Comparing HOPE Treatment and Present-Centered Therapy in Women Residing in Shelter with PTSD from Intimate Partner Violence.

Johnson DM; Palmieri PA; Zlotnick C; Johnson NL; Hoffman L; Holmes SC; Ceroni TL · Psychology of women quarterly · 2020 · Randomized active-comparator trial; author describes double-blind, but only assessor masking is explicitly role-verified

Original publication · Original publication · Original publication · Original publication · Original publication

Recorded summary

HOPE did not outperform an attention-matched PCT+ intervention on PTSD, IPV or secondary outcomes. Both active groups improved; no untreated control supports an absolute treatment-effect estimate.

Recorded limitations

Treatment providers knew protocols, and shared shelters permit contamination. Participant masking identity is not explicitly role-confirmed despite double-blind labeling. Missing-at-random models assume an unproven missingness mechanism; only 127/172 remained at 12 months. Active-comparator null results are not equivalence evidence.

quality
risk Of Bias
Randomization and masked clinical assessment strengthen comparison. Therapist allegiance/performance effects, possible contamination, unverified participant-role masking and follow-up attrition remain. No formal RoB or Jadad score assigned.
attrition
Assessment retention 154/172 post-shelter, 142/172 post-treatment, 135/172 at 6 months, 127/172 at 12 months. At 12 months 56/83 HOPE and 71/89 PCT+ contributed CAPS data. Treatment dropout 6/83 versus 5/89 is distinct from missing assessments. Black participants more often completed all assessments than White participants (81.6% vs 62.5%, p=.008).
confounding
ITT multivariate models use all available data under missing-at-random assumption; robust SEs and alpha<.01. Significant within-group change cannot separate psychotherapy, shelter services and time.
adverse Findings
Authors report no study-related serious adverse events, not zero adverse events overall. Ten participants were removed from assigned treatment for higher/different care: 6 HOPE, 4 PCT+; still invited to follow-up. Residual symptoms and some further abuse persisted.
transfer Limits
Women with recent IPV and diagnosed PTSD in US shelters; severe suicidal intent/plan, current psychosis, recent substance dependence, bipolar disorder or concurrent individual therapy were excluded.

    finding
    HOPE did not outperform an attention-matched PCT+ intervention on PTSD, IPV or secondary outcomes. Both active groups improved; no untreated control supports an absolute treatment-effect estimate.
    limitation
    Treatment providers knew protocols, and shared shelters permit contamination. Participant masking identity is not explicitly role-confirmed despite double-blind labeling.     cross Rationale
    Retain one cross: randomization and assessor masking verified. Reported double-blind label plus common naming does not independently verify the participant masking role. A two-cross proposal requires further role-specific primary confirmation; successful masking measurement is a separate limitation.
    quality
    risk Of Bias
    Randomization and masked clinical assessment strengthen comparison. Therapist allegiance/performance effects, possible contamination, unverified participant-role masking and follow-up attrition remain. No formal RoB or Jadad score assigned.
    attrition
    Assessment retention 154/172 post-shelter, 142/172 post-treatment, 135/172 at 6 months, 127/172 at 12 months. At 12 months 56/83 HOPE and 71/89 PCT+ contributed CAPS data. Treatment dropout 6/83 versus 5/89 is distinct from missing assessments. Black participants more often completed all assessments than White participants (81.6% vs 62.5%, p=.008).
    confounding
    ITT multivariate models use all available data under missing-at-random assumption; robust SEs and alpha<.01. Significant within-group change cannot separate psychotherapy, shelter services and time.
    adverse Findings
    Authors report no study-related serious adverse events, not zero adverse events overall. Ten participants were removed from assigned treatment for higher/different care: 6 HOPE, 4 PCT+; still invited to follow-up. Residual symptoms and some further abuse persisted.
    transfer Limits
    Women with recent IPV and diagnosed PTSD in US shelters; severe suicidal intent/plan, current psychosis, recent substance dependence, bipolar disorder or concurrent individual therapy were excluded.

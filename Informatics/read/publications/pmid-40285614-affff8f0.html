Oxytocin-Mediate Modulation of Splenic Immunosuppression in Chronic Social Stress Through Neuroendocrine Pathways

Zhang YS, Chen HC, Cao JX, Zhou SW, Ma YZ, Jing YH. · Advanced science (Weinheim, Baden-Wurttemberg, Germany) · 2025 · Chronic social-stress and splenic immune experiments in male mice

Original publication · Original publication

Recorded summary

Oxytocin-related signalling was associated with a shift toward regulatory T cells and M2-like macrophages, lower IL-6 and other inflammatory mediators, and higher IL-10, IL-4 and TGF-β. Denervation and receptor blockade disrupted this adaptive response.

Recorded limitations

The study concerns stress-associated immunosuppression and survival, not a general increase in immunity. Reduced effector-cell activity can be protective in one context and undesirable in another. Male-mouse findings do not establish a human intervention pathway. Access: Relevant full-text methods and results reviewed.

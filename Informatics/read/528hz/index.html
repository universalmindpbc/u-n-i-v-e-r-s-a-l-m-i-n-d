528 Hz sound and tuning: human trials, biomarkers and preclinical research

A small, active research field with promising findings for short-term stress and anxiety.

Trust Factor: 2 of 3 · four completed trials explicitly including 528 Hz

Oxytocin almost doubled within five minutes of listening to 528 Hz music in the 2018 study; the increase with 440 Hz music was not statistically significant. Other studies report reductions in anxiety, cortisol and blood pressure.

Several randomized trials now complement the earlier human and laboratory studies. The evidence remains preliminary: samples are generally small, blinding and reporting vary, follow-up is short, and some outcomes show no significant change.

Acute Music-Frequency Exposure Modulates Salivary Stress and Neurotrophic Markers in Young Adults: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Bozok ÜG, Böyük Özcan G, Bayraktar B, Özen D. · Brain and Behavior · 2026 · Randomized controlled trial; 162 students; three parallel groups

After 20 minutes of 528 Hz-labelled instrumental music, salivary CREB was higher and GRP78 lower than in the no-music group. Stroop performance did not differ. Table 1 gives 528 Hz and control the same BDNF significance label, despite the more positive abstract.

Research quality and limits: Exploratory salivary measures do not establish brain neuroplasticity, neuroprotection or experienced stress relief. Audio calibration and masking are insufficiently described. The abstract/table disagreement and inconsistent baseline-sampling description reduce confidence.

Effect of OM Chanting of 528Hz Frequency on Heart Rate Variability, Psychological Wellbeing, and Quality of Sleep in Patients of Hypertension: A Randomised Controlled Trial

Bhoot A, Chowdhury AB, Shah H, Vaishnav B. · Annals of Neurosciences · 2025 · Randomized controlled trial; 60 patients; 55 completed four weeks

Fifteen minutes of daily listening to 528 Hz OM chanting plus standard care produced promising blood-pressure and perceived-stress results against standard care. Pulse and systolic-pressure time-by-group interactions were significant; SDNN and RMSSD interactions were not. Between-group sleep and total wellbeing differences were not significant.

Research quality and limits: The study evaluates a chanting-and-listening practice. There is no matched recording at another tuning, and five participants were lost to follow-up. Multiple outcomes and differing statistical results limit a broad claim of improved autonomic function or sleep.

528Hz: The Sound Of Tranquillity And Its Effect On Anxiety

Heti M, Yeshaswini V. · Revista Electrónica de Veterinaria · 2024 · Reported randomized controlled experiment; 48 analysed; single session

A three-minute exposure described as a pure 528 Hz tone was compared with neutral reading. Mean state-anxiety scores fell by 4.63 points in the sound group and 0.83 in controls; the reported time-by-group interaction was p = .036.

Research quality and limits: Random sequence generation and concealment are unspecified. Participant-flow arithmetic, outlier handling, STAI descriptions and statistical reporting contain inconsistencies. No biomarkers or sustained outcomes were measured. Treat this as a low-confidence preliminary result.

Effects of a Sound-Based Meditative Intervention on Anxiety, Depression, Salivary Cortisol Levels, and Blood Pressure: A Randomized Controlled Trial

Ravikumar H, Sathyanarayanan S. · Cureus · 2026 · Randomized controlled trial; 100 adults; four weeks

Daily sound meditation incorporating recorded 528 Hz audio and acoustic instruments outperformed remotely delivered music on anxiety, depression, salivary cortisol and blood pressure. Adjusted differences included HADS anxiety −2.55 points (95% CI −3.62 to −1.48) and cortisol −2.54 nmol/L (−2.95 to −2.13).

Research quality and limits: In-person group meditation, instruments, equipment and extra home practice differed from the online control. Their contributions cannot be separated. Follow-up ended at four weeks, acoustic calibration was incomplete, and the registered POMS-SF outcome was omitted.

Effect of 528 Hz Music on the Endocrine System and Autonomic Nervous System

Akimoto K, Hu A, Yamaguchi T, Kobayashi H. · Health · 2018 · Small participant-masked crossover study

Oxytocin almost doubled within five minutes of listening to 528 Hz music. The increase with 440 Hz music was not statistically significant. Mean saliva levels rose from 37.57 to 73.58 pg/mL in the 528 Hz condition (p < 0.038). Cortisol also fell later after 528 Hz music.

Research quality and limits: Nine healthy adults took part. These short-term saliva findings need independent replication in larger samples.

Influence of various intensities of 528 Hz sound-wave in production of testosterone in rat’s brain and analysis of behavioral changes

Babayi Daylari T, Riazi GH, Pooyan S, Fathi E, Hedayati Katouli F. · Genes & genomics · 2019 · Preclinical rat experiment

Rat-brain and behavioural research examines 528 Hz exposure at different sound intensities.

Research quality and limits: Animal tissue and behavioural findings provide mechanistic context; they do not establish human treatment effects. The reported 100 dB condition is not a recommended human listening level.

Cell-culture research examines viability and oxidative-stress measures after ethanol and 528 Hz sound exposure.

Neural Coding of High-Frequency Tones

NASA · Auditory coding reference; not a stress-reduction trial

This archival auditory-coding paper uses a 528 Hz reference in a pitch-comparison context.

Research quality and limits: Retained as auditory-science background. It supplies no evidence of therapeutic stress reduction at 528 Hz.

Study On Impact of Sound Frequency on Human Well Being Using Randomized Block Design

Unhale SI, Khairnar SL, Joshi MA. · Journal of Advanced Zoology · 2023 · Reported randomized block experiment; RCT eligibility unresolved

The report includes 57 participants aged 17–20 and exposures labelled 379, 430 and 528 Hz. Mean ratings for calmness, focus and stress relief were higher at 430 Hz than at 528 Hz. A pooled before-and-after heart-rate reduction from 80.56 to 79.25 bpm does not separate the frequencies.

Research quality and limits: The allocation and repeated-exposure structure are unclear; group sizes, sequence concealment and frequency-specific heart-rate comparisons are not adequately reported. A pooled change cannot identify the effect of 528 Hz. Randomized block design is a randomization method, but the reporting is insufficient to confirm a qualifying controlled trial here.

Instruments for sound therapy vs. music therapy for teenager's emotional development

Pomazan VM. · Proceedings of ICSIE 2023 · ACM · 2023 · Author-reported randomized controlled conference study; 28 adolescents

The paper describes randomized groups receiving a sound device, active music therapy or control. It reports improvements in emotional self-ratings, without a significant difference between the two therapy groups.

Research quality and limits: Only eight participants received the sound device. The described frequencies are 240–500 Hz with a 410–450 Hz base; 528 Hz appears in the conceptual Solfeggio mapping, rather than as a verified delivered tone. This is related frequency-family context with uncertain transfer to 528 Hz listening.

Real-Time Analysis of Therapeutic Board Game and Sound Therapy Effects on Adolescent Mental Health Using Facial Emotion and Stress Detection

Khalisha S, Alfarezy K, Villanda TM, Herlambang AF, Rahaman MS, Fikry M. · IEEE International Conference on Activity and Behavior Computing · 2026 · Conference publication; study design awaiting full-text verification

The publication identity is verified through publisher-deposited Crossref metadata. The title concerns board-game and sound interventions with facial-emotion and stress detection.

Research quality and limits: Full methods were inaccessible during this review. Sample size, random allocation, delivered frequencies and treatment findings remain unverified. Retained as a research lead; no RCT or efficacy claim is assigned.

Efficacy, Treatment Characteristics, and Biopsychological Mechanisms of Music-Listening Interventions in Reducing Pain (MINTREP): Study Protocol of a Three-Armed Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial

Feneberg AC, Kappert MB, Maidhof RM, Doering BK, Olbrich D, Nater UM. · Frontiers in Psychiatry · 2020 · Randomized controlled trial protocol; no outcome results in this publication

This protocol plans 90 participants and ten listening sessions comparing frequency-filtered, unmodified and self-selected music. It cites the earlier 444 Hz-tuned piano study and offers useful methods for examining music, stress and pain.

Research quality and limits: The proposed filtering spans 50–4,000 Hz. This publication supplies no completed outcome evidence and does not specify a 528 Hz treatment arm. Its planned randomization cannot be counted as a completed 528 Hz RCT.

Groups overlap. The youth discovery group combines source-defined youth/young people, adolescents and young-adult age indexing. Its 170 records include 63 with a youth focus and 107 with youth coverage but no established youth focus in the checked descriptions. The interface distinguishes these states. Twenty-seven reported age ranges remain visible, including the iBobbly study's 18–35 range. Eligibility ranges are labelled separately and do not alone create age-group assignments.

NLM age indexing informs the classification alongside explicit source wording. Its [PubMed age filters](https://www.nlm.nih.gov/databases/download/pubmed_medline.html) and [Young Adult descriptor](https://meshb.nlm.nih.gov/record/ui?ui=D055815) describe the indexing conventions. Source-defined older-adult and youth thresholds can differ. No universal age cutoff is imposed on a publication's terminology.

Editorial checks remove Child indexing from the adult childhood-trauma trial `pmid-32892758`; its participants are adults. Adult indexing in `pmid-20117669` relates to parent reports and does not establish an adult intervention cohort. Kinship words, school affiliation, occupation, animal ages and unresolved source identities do not establish a human age group. Null youth findings remain discoverable.

The source and editorial audit retains each basis, metadata hash and pending review status. The public index contains age groups, source links, reported ranges and youth coverage, with no calibration or approval workings in the visitor interface. The original 50 community approvals do not automatically approve new age tags.

## Universal One

The existing research Worker receives a read-only navigation tool built from the same community and age indexes as the website. It supplies exact visible-library counts, combined community/age/collection matches, filter links and evidence limits. Universal One can explain direct/contextual coverage, Related · inferred, mixed-age coverage and Age not specified. The browser passes bounded active-filter context.

This update changes the assistant's supplied knowledge and tools; it does not fine-tune model weights or change the publication/full-text database. The visible library and the retrieval database are explicitly distinguished. Study-specific clinical claims still require publication retrieval. The existing staging Worker configuration and Panthur website workflow remain in use.

The Dot brief specifies the later automatic handoff from an immutable Universal Mind approval record to a versioned assistant knowledge refresh, staged retrieval checks and rollback. That future approval-event pipeline is not activated in this release. Research approval does not authorize production publication. Dot delivery and its actual local-computer/Panthur access remain unverified; no continuous research run or recurring schedule has started.

# Proposed methods and safety corrections for active review

Prepared 2026-10-30. These are proposals against **batch-8bd70e21e48d9a70**. The staged batch and original worker files have not been edited. These five publications represent four study families; this work adds no new study or participant count. All human-review states remain pending.

**Nguvu, PMID 34143803:** The full report narrows the abstract's apparent psychological benefit. Its baseline-adjusted complete-case model included 272 of 311 enrolled women: depression mean difference −0.14 (95% CI −0.30 to 0.03), anxiety −0.12 (−0.30 to 0.06), and PTSD −0.18 (−0.36 to 0.00). No IPV outcome showed clear benefit. The favorable PTSD result among treatment completers is a selected, nonrandom comparison and cannot replace the randomized estimate. In an exploratory subgroup with higher baseline IPV, subsequent IPV frequency was higher with Nguvu: mean difference 0.90 (0.38 to 1.42). This exploratory signal is affected by baseline imbalance, multiple testing and differential intervention completion; it does **not** establish that treatment caused violence. It must nevertheless remain visible. Three adverse events were reviewed and judged unlikely related to participation; they were not zero adverse events. One participant withdrew because she feared neighbors discovering participation. Only 92/158 intervention participants attended at least six sessions; more severe IPV predicted lower completion. Follow-up was only nine weeks after enrollment, so durable benefit is unestablished.

**Ethiopia PST-IPV, PMID 37889918:** The full report documents a serious allocation limitation: **17/52** women reporting psychological/emotional IPV were wrongly routed through the linked trial's two-arm table, making them ineligible for assignment to PST-IPV. Only 35 entered the intended three-arm process; final groups were 25/12/15. The authors also flag possible nonrandom allocation from baseline IPV differences. Twelve of 39 follow-ups were performed early; follow-up was missing for 13/52, with nonresponders younger and apparently more symptomatic. This supports feasibility learning, not a claim of comparative efficacy. **One serious adverse event was an assault by a participant's husband; group and causal attribution are not reported.** Two obstetric complications were recorded among 29 women who had delivered. Therapists sometimes failed to respond when women raised IPV, and risk/confidentiality discussion needed improvement. The conclusion's description of safe delivery must not be paraphrased as an absence of harm. There is no long-term outcome assessment.

**WEAVE, PMIDs 23598181 and 33303427:** These are the same randomized cohort, not two independent replications. At 24 months, only 166/272 responded; no quality-of-life, mental-health, IPV, depression, anxiety or PTSD outcome showed a significant comparative benefit. The secondary depression advantage reported at 12 months did not persist. Only 67/137 women offered counselling attended any, usually one session. The 2013 abstract's statement that no adverse events were recorded should be accompanied by the same-trial harms paper's qualitative reports of emotional distress and retraumatization. These are harms of research participation, not demonstrated excess harms caused by counselling. The accessible harms abstract provides neither event counts nor causal attribution sufficient to quantify risk.

**HOPE172, PMID 34305273:** Full methods strengthen the design appraisal but do not establish superiority to PCT+. Both active arms improved; no between-group outcome difference was significant. At 12 months 127/172 were retained (56/83 HOPE, 71/89 PCT+). Ten participants stopped assigned treatment because they needed different or higher care (6 HOPE, 4 PCT+); the authors reported no **study-related serious** adverse events, a narrower claim than no adverse events. The author labels the study double-blind, explicitly masks outcome assessors, and describes common treatment/workbook naming to conceal allocation identity. A separate explicit statement that participants were unaware of allocation and a masking-success assessment are absent. Under conservative verification, retain **one cross** pending adjudication of participant masking; do not infer two crosses solely from the shared names. Therapists delivered both identifiable protocols.

# Sound assessment source check

22 October 2026. Scope: the new expandable Sound assessment in Universal Research. Existing Universal Mind B assessment retained with Ben’s approval; this is not a new formal GRADE review. The key-study selections come from the existing I AM portal. Publication abstracts/full text were checked for the newly written summaries, rather than treating the portal text as independent confirmation.

| Publication | Checked source | Content boundary applied |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Music therapy for the treatment of anxiety, 2025 | [PubMed 40547443](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40547443/), DOI 10.1016/j.eclinm.2025.103293 | 51 studies; self-reported anxiety improvement, small nonsignificant physiological result. No claim that all music listening has the same effect. |
| Music interventions for PTSD, 2024 | [PMC12244202](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244202/), DOI 10.1016/j.xjmad.2024.100053 | 14 studies, 13 reporting reduced symptoms; nine feasibility studies. Small samples and limited controls constrain causal interpretation. |
| Brain wave modulation and EEG power changes, 2024 | [PubMed 39004412](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39004412/), DOI 10.1016/j.neuroscience.2024.07.014 | Abstract also retrieved from Europe PMC API, query EXT_ID:39004412 AND SRC:MED, resultType=core. 28 participants, four-minute exposures. Isochronic-derived stimulation is not interchangeable with conventional fixed-carrier tones. EEG effects do not demonstrate clinical efficacy or transcription. |
| Effect of 528 Hz Music, 2018 | [Publisher full text](https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=87146), DOI 10.4236/health.2018.109088 | Nine participants, brief music exposures. Reported within-condition changes do not establish a between-condition advantage. Salivary oxytocin is not OXTR transcription. |
| Theta binaural beat therapy review, 2026 | [PubMed 42349368](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42349368/) and publisher abstract, DOI 10.1016/j.explore.2026.103471 | 13 RCTs; four contributed to pooled pain result, with low certainty. Anxiety/cognition too heterogeneous to pool. No established PTSD efficacy claim. |
| Recreational music-making and stress gene expression, 2005 | [PubMed 15668624](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15668624/) | 32 participants, blood expression of 45 immune-response genes. Preliminary intervention-specific findings, not a general mechanism. |
| Music and buccal gene expression, 2025 | [Frontiers full text](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2025.1622816/full), DOI 10.3389/fnagi.2025.1622816 | Exploratory oral-tissue expression differences. No inference of equivalent brain changes or therapeutic efficacy. |

All seven publications are already present in the research library. This addition changes no underlying records, quotations, trust scores or totals. Newly authored article summaries are paraphrases without quotation marks.

The internal single-participant prototype observation is not used as a clinical anchor in this assessment. No existing paper or chapter was removed. Anxiety/PTSD remain the initial clinical focus; other research missions are welcome.

The B grade is scoped to the wider clinical/physiological evidence. Existing gene-expression findings and future OXTR investigations are discussed separately without a new letter grade. The medical crosses remain the existing study-design shorthand.

Ben confirmed the public B label as “Positive systematic evidence” on 22 October 2026. The expanded guide retains the agreed A/B/C definitions and “Grades describe the research basis, not a medical recommendation.” The earlier shorthand has been removed.

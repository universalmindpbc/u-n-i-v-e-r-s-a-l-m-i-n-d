# Universal Mind Lab — creative and development brief

**Version:** 0.3 · **Date:** 2 November 2026 · **Owner:** Ben Korbel  
**Status:** Working concept for discussion, collaboration and staged visual development. Architectural engineering, operating arrangements and named partnerships remain to be established.

## 1. Purpose

Create an interactive architectural world and partnership pitch for Universal Mind Lab: a travelling Australian installation that brings the IAM portal into a physical environment through music, fashion, mindfulness, learning, food, play and gathering.

The primary audience is people aged 16–24, with the wider community welcome. The experience combines a main pavilion with smaller surrounding facilities, nature, Moroso Tropicália seating and individual music discovery stations.

The proposed touring logistics centre on a Linfox road train. Local builders and collaborators help create each site. Where the host community wants to retain facilities, an agreed pavilion or gathering space may remain as a playground, mental health and mindfulness resource, or other community use.

The web experience makes the proposal tangible: visitors explore the setting, follow its assembly, discover each activity and see what may remain after the touring programme departs.

## 3. Community collaboration and a lasting public space

Community collaboration is part of the design process from the beginning. It includes decisions about location, layout, materials, activities, accessibility, operation and the future of any retained structures.

Recommended process to develop with Ben and the host community:

1. Establish local partners and participants, including young people, relevant community organisations and local builders. Engage the relevant Traditional Owners and local First Nations participants as appropriate to the place and project.
2. Agree how participants contribute, how their work is resourced, and how decisions are made and recorded.
3. Develop the site brief and material options together. Leave space for the community to reshape the layout, programme and retained facilities.
4. Review visual studies and practical prototypes with the people who will use and maintain the space.
5. Agree what leaves with the touring Lab, what remains, who operates it, and how maintenance and eventual removal or reuse are funded.

Local availability is one input to material selection. Assess transport, durability, repair, weather performance, sourcing, embodied impacts and reuse before making environmental claims. A familiar material appearance in a concept image does not establish the final construction system.

## 4. Architectural direction

### First version: one main vault and surrounding facilities

Use the single Droneport shell as the main architectural anchor. Preserve the sense of a continuous, light-looking vaulted form, open edges, daylight and connection to the ground. Resolve actual span, floor area and capacity through a later dimensioned layout and professional design.

Food and play occupy smaller satellite pavilions. Individual listening stations sit in a quieter area with deliberate acoustic and weather protection. Practical facilities include arrival, accessible circulation, toilets, water, power, storage and service access; their exact provision is still open.

### Future version: permanent multi-vault centre

R06 is the preferred future reference: the long landscape composition with approximately five or six visible arches and a flag. Carry its rhythm, horizon and civic presence into a later permanent-centre study. The initial touring/community concept uses a single main vault.

### Unifying the main and satellite forms

Develop a shared design language through related curves, transitions to the ground, a locally informed material palette, modest service elements and planting suited to the actual region. The food and play pavilions retain their fluid ZHA-derived silhouettes, with simplified continuous shells, the same colour and material/surface family as the main vault, and no oval windows, cellular apertures or elaborate ribs. Architectural authorship and proposed collaboration remain explicit. Do not present a speculative adaptation as an approved Foster or ZHA design.

The final palette is a collaboration outcome. Warm masonry in the references is a visual starting point, not a locked material specification for every site or pavilion.

### Site and landscape character

Seek a broad, lush green open space backed by a river or creek and a forest/bush setting. Ben's preferred composition has bush along two boundaries, water along a third, and an open arrival edge. Preserve clear views and generous lawn between the main vault, food, play and listening areas. Concentrate substantial planting at the perimeter; avoid the initial sketch's dense scrub, rocks and trees within the central activity areas.

This is a site-selection character brief. An actual site, environmental conditions, suitable separation from the waterway and accessible routes remain to be established with the community and design team. The sketch's water position does not establish a surveyed boundary or approved setback.

In the open lawn arrangement, suspended Tropicália seats need independent support frames or another specifically designed support system. Do not assume nearby mature trees or use transparent display pylons as structural supports for seats.

### Proposed technology and engineering collaboration

Ben intends to work with CSIRO and Arup alongside the proposed architectural collaboration. Record them as prospective collaborators. Their workstreams, team involvement and commitments are yet to be agreed. Explore technical and material questions with the relevant partners while keeping community collaboration active throughout design decisions.

### Reference identification

- **R02–R08:** Droneport photographs and visualisations. R03 informs the assembly story; R07 is the primary single-vault form reference; R06 expresses the future permanent ambition.
- **R21–R23:** Striatus, a 3D-printed concrete footbridge by ZHA and ETH Zürich's Block Research Group. Use it as a component, geometry and reassembly reference. It is a bridge, and converting its ideas into a pavilion requires design work.
- **R24–R25:** Volu dining pavilion. Use its garden setting, canopy and organic detailing as references for smaller facilities. The pictured table and benches are separate from the decision to use Tropicália as the main seating collection.

## 7. Apple Music listening pylons

### Physical and visual intent

The listening stations are transparent tubular pylons emerging from the earth, clustered at varied heights around Tropicália seating. They have circular or softly organic cross-sections and a sense of mineral growth.

The three creative references are:

- **Minority Report:** responsive imagery appearing within transparent space.
- **Future Apple Music:** restrained typography, artist discovery, album imagery and intuitive controls.
- **Superman's Crystal Cave:** an extraordinary environment growing from the earth.

Album artwork and controls may follow a curved surface or appear suspended within a column. The accepted geometry is volumetric and tubular. Exclude rectangular tablet-shaped pylons, flat billboard screens and a generic meditation-room aesthetic. Transparency, sunlight readability and interaction technology need later hardware investigation.

Use unobtrusive headphone storage, reachable controls and discreet wiring. Interface movement remains subtle and user controlled. Each pod is a composition of seating, soft furnishings and one or more pylons; exact pylon heights, counts and spacing remain open.

### Audio and music

AKG K812 is the preferred reference headphone. Its open-back design makes ambient noise and sound leakage important to the layout. Evaluate the full listening chain, including the source recording, supported Apple playback device, wired output and headphones.

Apple documents Dolby Atmos listening through wired headphones with the appropriate Always On setting. Open-back construction and price alone do not establish spatial accuracy. A later physical listening prototype is needed to assess the actual experience; silent browser checks cannot establish sound quality.

Use curated released Spatial Audio recordings as the initial content basis. Unreleased music, artist previews and premieres remain part of the proposed partnership request, with arrangements to be established with Apple and the relevant artists or rights holders. Event playback arrangements are part of that partnership work.

Treat the interactive website's listening feature as a visual/interaction prototype until its actual playback path is verified. A custom browser interface is not proof that Apple Music Dolby Atmos plays through that interface.

### Proposed partnership roles

- Apple Music: music discovery, curation, potential premieres and programme collaboration.
- Apple Retail: a possible participant in the physical experience and devices.
- AKG / HARMAN: K812 headphones and evaluation of the listening experience.
- Moroso: furniture, accurate digital assets and possible design collaboration.

These are proposals. The brief does not claim confirmed sponsorship, access, endorsement or supply.

## 8. Light-rain operation

The installation is intended to remain operational in light rain. Develop the roof, rain-shedding details, drainage, exposed edges, floor surfaces, sheltered routes and equipment protection as one system.

Retractable screens and lightweight covered connections are options for study. Their effect on wind loading, appearance, access and acoustics needs design review. All occupied programme areas, including the listening stations, need a workable rain arrangement. A sculptural open canopy alone does not confirm rain protection.

Show both open/fine-weather and light-rain configurations in the later 3D world. Operating limits remain subject to the actual architectural and engineering design.

## 9. Touring, construction and community retention

### Touring installation

The road train arrives at the service edge, equipment is unloaded, the site is assembled and fitted out, the programme operates, then touring components depart. The one-week setup and one-week pack-down ambitions need assessment against the selected system, site preparation, crew, equipment and local conditions.

The Droneport prototype's published record describes four weeks on site. It does not validate a seven-day masonry build or repeated dismantling. Keep the programme target explicit while exploring systems that can support it.

### Community-retained installation

Where the host community agrees, selected structures remain as public gathering, play, mental health or mindfulness resources. Establish the retained asset list, ownership, operation, maintenance funding, access and end-of-term arrangements with local partners. The proposed 12-month period depends on the actual site and permissions.

### Assembly animation

For the initial visual story, animate the road train arriving, unloading components, local assembly, installation of services and furnishing. The first animation is explanatory. Do not imply that a completed masonry vault folds out of a truck or that a concept sequence is a validated construction method. A verified method can replace the placeholder sequence later.

### Free membership and supported entry

Membership and event attendance are intended to be free. Applications help identify interest in music and fashion, allocate limited places, and establish the support that each participant needs.

Proposed entry routes:

1. A participating headspace service receives applications or makes referrals.
2. Participating NSW schools nominate interested students through a volunteer senior teacher, principal or agreed school wellbeing contact. A short survey is one possible entry step.
3. Priority intentions include young people experiencing economic disadvantage, communities facing additional barriers, special support needs, or a need for mental health and community connection.

Ben's reference is the participation model of Global Citizen concerts: free, limited tickets with positive actions and an allocation process. This is an analogy, not a confirmed Global Citizen partnership or a settled auction or ballot mechanism. Define the eventual allocation method with partners and young people. Recommended design direction: accessible participation options and transparent allocation that does not rank personal worth, reward disclosure of sensitive health information, or make clinical support a competition. No scoring or eligibility algorithm is authorised for this prototype.

Ben's initial support-interest examples include anxiety, PTSD and occasional depression. He is concerned about accepting needs beyond the team's safe capacity, including acute presentations. These examples are planning context, not validated clinical eligibility categories. Clinical partners need to define individual assessment, consent, available care, escalation, referral and follow-up. Diagnosis or duration alone is not used as an automatic exclusion rule in the website.

The proposed public wording is: **Free membership. A supported application process through participating youth and education partners, with places matched to programme capacity and available support.**

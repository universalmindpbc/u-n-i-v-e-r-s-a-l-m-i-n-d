LIKE YOURSELF

What we choose to wear can change how we feel in our bodies.

Human research suggests that personally expressive clothing, satisfying garment fit and comfortable athletic apparel can influence state self-esteem, body appreciation, body confidence and positive affect. Controlled fabric studies also demonstrate that softness, pressure and textile quality produce measurable differences in subjective experience and brain response.

Self-selected clothing can support how we feel in our bodies.

Our testable hypothesis

Editorially, we can call the experience empowerment. Scientifically, we measure it through perceived agency, state self-esteem, body appreciation, comfort and positive affect.

“In consenting adults, wearing a self-selected, well-fitting James Bondage [ health ] intimate garment designed for tactile comfort, adjustability and personally meaningful self-expression will produce short-term improvements in state body appreciation, perceived agency, comfort and positive affect, compared with a materially and fit-matched neutral garment.”

This hypothesis brings together established research on self-selection, body appreciation, agency, tactile comfort, fit and affect. The complete James Bondage [ health ] proposition is intended for future testing.

Material Intelligence

KNOW WHAT THE
FABRIC TELLS US.
KNOW WHAT IT
DOESN’T.

We do not guess what is in a garment. We translate what is declared, verify the standards behind it, explain the material in context and show where the evidence comes from.

01

NYLON IS NOT PFAS

Nylon is a synthetic fibre. PFAS are a broad family of fluorinated chemicals that may be used in some finishes. One does not prove the other.

02

PFAS ARE NOT MICROPLASTICS

PFAS persistence and synthetic microfibre shedding are separate questions. A garment can raise one, both or neither.

03

NATURAL IS NOT AUTOMATICALLY UNTREATED

Cotton and wool can offer comfort and renewable-fibre benefits, while dyes, finishes, sourcing and processing still matter.

04

PERFORMANCE CLAIMS DESERVE ATTENTION

Water-, oil-, stain- and leak-resistant claims are stronger signals to investigate than the word “synthetic” alone.

05

DECLARATIONS AND STANDARDS COUNT

Brand chemical policies, GOTS, OEKO-TEX, bluesign and supplier controls are meaningful evidence when their scope is clear.

06

TESTING IS AN EXTRA LAYER

Independent laboratory testing can add confidence. It is not a reasonable prerequisite for describing every responsible brand.

PFAS deserve particular care because of their environmental persistence and evidence linking exposure to health effects. Synthetic microfibre shedding is a different, still-evolving exposure question. We keep them separate so concern does not become misinformation.

PFAS + finish position

Reported separately from the stars. “No PFAS-type finish indicated” means no water-, oil- or stain-repellent PFAS treatment is advertised or identified. It is not a laboratory-tested PFAS-free claim.

MATERIAL NOTES＋

Evidence state

Declared comes from a brand or product page. Verified standard names an applicable certification or published control. Inferred characteristic describes what the product type suggests without inventing a chemical claim.

Tulle

Tulle describes an open-net fabric structure, not one fibre or treatment. It may use nylon, polyester, silk or other fibres. Synthetic tulle does not itself indicate PFAS.

Neoprene + recycled synthetics

Neoprene is synthetic rubber, not PFAS. Recycled synthetics can reduce virgin-fibre demand but remain synthetic and may shed microfibres. We describe each issue on its own terms.

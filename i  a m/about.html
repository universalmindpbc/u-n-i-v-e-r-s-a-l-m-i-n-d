OVERVIEW

I AM / THE EXPERIENCE

From anxiety to peace of mind, through music.

ANXIETY PEACE OF MIND

I AM is a free online mental health resource for young people, framed through the lens of electronic music, fashion and online support, together as one.

I AM brings electronic music, fashion and human connection together as a safe refuge in the moment.

When anxiety or crisis arrives, we can’t always reach a doctor or therapist straight away. I AM is designed to be there in that gap.

  • Watch live electronic music videos, DJs, fashion runways and festivals together as one. #livestream
  • Feeling especially under the weather. Try 10 minutes of our EMDR Nervous System Reset, all without interrupting your music television experience. Get your friends, new and old, to join in, together as one. #livestream
  • All while, optionally, chatting online together in our audio rooms. #livestream
  • Receive care packages, delivered to your door, courtesy of us, for when you need it the most - ugg boots, tea and some merch from your favourite artist, together as one. #nexthour

For support, our policy is this:

  • Real Time - Peer and Mentor Support 24/7 via I AM audio rooms #livestream
  • 1hr - An I AM care package delivered to your door in an hour, with love - thanks to Uber X, Harris Farm and Ugg. #nexthour
  • Same Day - Access to a therapist the same day for additional support via Telehealth #sameday

We envision partnerships with Lifeline, Headspace and Blackdog, promoting their existing support pathways, together as one.

01

PROBLEM

YOUNG VETERANS / AUSTRALIA

The barrier is not one thing.

Barriers to mental health care within Australia, especially for young veterans returning from active duty, include stigma, access and cost.

Stigma
“I am too embarrassed to seek help. It’s only mental health.”
Access
“It’s too far to travel to find a specialist or therapist.”
Cost
“I can’t afford it.”

A direct response to Orygen + Phoenix Australia’s “The Next Post: Young people transitioning from military service and their mental health”.

02

SOLUTION

CULTURE / ACCESS / ZERO COST

Place support where trust already lives.

Proposed solutions to mental health barriers within Australia, especially for young veterans returning from active duty, are entertainment, digital access and free participation.

Entertainment
Place mental health within the cultural spaces people trust and where they already spend their time.
Digital
Include more therapies, research layers and support pathways online.
Free
Remove price as a barrier to showing up.

RESEARCH

CLINICAL HUMILITY / EVIDENCE IN PUBLIC

A proposal to test what the experience changes.

Universal Mind’s I AM technology begins its life as a research study. We are exploring whether 10 minutes of optional EMDR layers within livestream music television may modulate physiological and transcriptional pathways associated with mental health.

TECHNICAL STUDY FRAMEWORK OPEN APPENDIX +
01 / VISUAL

Saccadic eye movement

An optional EMDR visual layer using animation and video to support bilateral eye movement.

02 / SOUND

Isochronic + continuous audio

An exploratory sound layer proposing to examine high-theta activity, baroreflex central nervous system measures and oxytocin receptor pathways.

PROPOSED RANDOMISED CONTROLLED STUDY PROGRESSION

  1. PROTOTYPE1person
  2. MVP5people
  3. PILOT10people
  4. STUDY50people
  5. SCALE100+people

Prototype, MVP and pilot stages focus on heart rate, blood pressure and self-report before any investment in genomic mRNA sequencing.

PRELIMINARY INTERNAL OBSERVATION / NOT CLINICAL PROOF

33% ± reported reduction Preliminary observations recorded a repeatable reduction in heart rate and blood pressure within 5–10 minutes for individual audience members with severe hypertension and during cancer recovery. This observation has not yet been established by a controlled clinical trial and is presented as the reason for seeking rigorous independent research.

Universal Mind does not intend to replace existing care pathways. I AM provides free digital mental health support for at-risk communities while creating referral pathways to psychotherapy, psychology, EMDR and primary care.

The proposed genomic focus begins with anxiety and trauma among young Australian veterans returning from service. Research and clinical conclusions must remain with qualified independent partners.
